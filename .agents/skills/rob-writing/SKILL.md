# Rob's writing

Read `like-rob` when available and the private `~/Documents/agent-artifacts/robertjw-discoverability/editorial/voice.md` when present.
Use Rob's current interview answers and edits as the strongest voice evidence. Existing published writing in `public/index.html` is a starting sample, not proof Rob endorses every stylistic habit in it.

Write as a practicing engineer explaining a decision to another person. Lead with the concrete problem, show the choice and reason, then explain the result and limits. Keep technical details that help someone repeat or assess the work.
Use plain words, varied sentence lengths, and connected paragraphs. Preserve Rob's opinions and uncertainty. Tighten speech without flattening it into corporate copy.
Reuse one concrete example through the explanation. Prefer actual constraints, code, observations, and tradeoffs over broad predictions.
Avoid promotional openings, forced three-part lists, formulaic conclusions, em dashes, rhetorical question-and-answer tricks, and phrases like "delve", "leverage", "game-changing", or "in today's evolving landscape".
Do not add headings to every short paragraph or turn every sentence into a bullet.

First-person experience needs Rob's statement or verified work evidence; an assistant's earlier suggestion is not something Rob did. Never invent a personal anecdote, outcome, quote, benchmark, client detail, or motivation. Mark a missing answer in private editorial notes and ask Rob.
Verify changing technical claims against current primary sources. Link evidence near the claim. Separate Rob's opinion from externally testable fact.
Private work may suggest an article, but customer data, internal incidents, private links, credentials, and unpublished company plans must not leak into public-ready copy. Use a generic example or flag the specific passage for Rob's review.

Before delivering a draft, read it aloud mentally and remove sentences that could appear unchanged in any AI article. Check that it answers the intended reader's question early and includes something learned from Rob's actual work.
Keep title, suggested slug, short description, intended audience, and remaining fact checks in separate editorial notes. No keyword stuffing or padded word-count targets.
Update private `voice.md` only with clear preferences, Rob's edits, and short attributed examples. Label inferred preferences provisional. Never treat an unreviewed generated draft as a new voice sample.
The writing schedule creates private drafts; publishing requires Rob's instruction for the concrete article.
